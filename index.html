      III.

      The method

      Every number on this page comes from a small, inspectable model. Here is all of it.

      Why a katta

      The sociologist Ray Oldenburg called them third places: not home, not work, but somewhere you can drop in without an invitation, that costs little, is close by, has regulars, and where talk is the main activity. In Pune the word for one is कट्टा, a roadside ledge where people sat and argued for decades, and which road traffic has largely erased. This lab asks what is lost, and for whom, when such places go.

      The people

      Two synthetic groups: long-time residents, housed mostly in the inner quarters and arriving with ties to three neighbours, and newcomers, housed mostly at the edges and arriving with nobody. Each person has a budget and a sociability, drawn from clipped normals. None of this is calibrated to real data.

      An evening

      Each person goes out with probability equal to their sociability, then picks a place, or home, by a multinomial logit. The utility of place j is

      U = −βd·d/250 − 3βp·price·(1−budget) + 1.2·conv
  + 0.9·ln(1+habit) + 0.6·min(3, friends there)
  + 2βh·(share like me − ½) − 2.5·max(0, crowd/cap − 0.85)

      with staying home worth 0.4. Crowd size and composition are a place's reputation, an exponential moving average of past evenings, which stops the whole city stampeding to yesterday's empty room.

      Ties

      At each place, every attendee holds 1 + ⌊4 · sociability · conv⌋ conversations with random others there. A conversation updates tie strength s ← s + g(1 − s), with g = 0.22·conv for people of the same group and g = 0.22·conv·(1 − friction) across groups. Overcrowding dilutes conviviality by capacity / crowd. Ties decay by 3.5% a night; habits by 5%.

      Measures

      Belonging per person is 1 − exp(−(0.55·strong + 0.18·weak + 0.06·attachment)): close ties matter most, acquaintances a little, being a regular somewhere a little more. Mixing is the share of ties that cross groups divided by 2p(1−p), the share expected by chance. The dissimilarity index asks what share of one group would have to switch places for both to be spread alike. Clustering is the average local clustering coefficient of the tie network.

      Experiments

      Replicate k of A and of B use the same seed: the same people, the same coin flips where the scenarios agree. This is common random numbers, a classic Monte Carlo variance-reduction trick. Outcomes are averaged over each run's last ten evenings, and the paired differences are resampled 2,000 times for a percentile interval.

      What this is not

      Not a forecast, not a measurement of any real neighbourhood, and not a claim about any real group. It is a thinking tool for asking which design choices plausibly matter, and for seeing how strongly the answer depends on assumptions you can change.
