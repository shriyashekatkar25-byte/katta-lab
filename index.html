Kattaकट्टा

A laboratory for third places

Where does a city spend its evenings, and who ends up meeting whom? Build a small synthetic city of chai stalls, reading rooms, ghats, a single-screen cinema and cafés, watch three hundred and sixty residents choose where to go, and run paired Monte Carlo experiments on what a new place, a price, or the monsoon does to belonging.

कट्टा katta, n. (Marathi): a raised ledge or platform, and by extension the regular spot where friends gather to talk. Ray Oldenburg called such places third places: neither home (first) nor work (second).

        05

        Method

        Everything the city does, in one page.

        Residents

        A population of synthetic residents (default 360), 40% newcomers. Each has a home near one of six fictional neighbourhoods; by default most live on their cohort's side of the river (mixed housing 0.35). Budgets and sociability are drawn from the same distributions for both cohorts, so differences you see come from taste, geography and feedback, not from built-in inequality.

        Choosing an evening

        A resident goes out with probability equal to their sociability (×0.7 in rain), then picks among open places and home by a multinomial logit, P(j) ∝ exp(Uj/T), with

        U = 2.2·appeal(type, cohort)
  − βdist·3·distance/diagonal
  − βprice·cost·(1.2 − budget)
  + βhabit·0.6·ln(1 + habit)
  + βhomophily·2·(share of own cohort yesterday − ½)
  − βcrowd·max(0, load/capacity − 1)
  − 2.5·[rain ∧ outdoor]
Uhome = 0.4

        Cohort tastes (appeal) are explicit assumptions in src/lib/model.ts: settled residents lean to the tapri, newcomers to the café, both like the ghat and reading room.

        Conversations and ties

        Inside a place each visitor starts a few conversations (tapri and café 3, ghat 2, reading room and cinema 1) with someone present, drawn at random with a mild pull toward people they already know. Each conversation adds 1 to a symmetric tie; ties fade by the decay rate every evening and vanish below 0.1. A tie of 2.5 or more counts as strong.

        Measures

        • Belonging: per resident 1 − exp(−Σ v²/(v²+4) / 5), averaged.
        • Bridging ties: share of strong ties that cross cohorts.
        • Mixing ratio: bridging ÷ its value if ties ignored cohort (2pq·n/(n−1)).
        • Isolation: share with no strong tie.
        • Place segregation: Duncan dissimilarity of the cohorts across places tonight.
        • Evenings out: share of residents out tonight.

        Experiments

        A and B run on the same seeds (common random numbers). Each world is summarised by its last ten evenings. Intervals for A and B are t-intervals; the difference uses a paired bootstrap (2,000 resamples); effect size is paired Cohen's d.

        This is a toy. No parameter is calibrated to any real city, neighbourhood or community, and no result here is a finding about real people.
